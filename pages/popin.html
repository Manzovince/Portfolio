Pop-in

When a child is born, two adults are transformed into parents. In a way, it is the child who makes the parents. Becoming a parent is life changing, in every way possible.

From happiness to frustration, we want the best for our children, and this can very quickly become stressful. Elodie Hughes, mother of 4 children and entrepreneur, has put together a small team to find a solution.

Research

Child development is a complex process. Parents need to learn a lot of things, and the web is a wide source of knowledge, but informations often contradicts. To provide clear and concise facts and tips to parents, and in the new era of human-like interaction with technology, we found that creating a chatbot was a fun and powerful way to educate.

We made a corpus of the most asked questions asked by parents, and there are a lot of subjects about babies: alimentation, health, sleep... Babies need a lot of sleep (approx. 20 hours per day as a newborn) and parents too. But it often doesn't work as we want, because babies don't have the same sleep cycle, and they need to embody the circadian rythm of day and night.

We decided to give only science-based facts, and link them with practical tips and advices when it was possible.

Design and build

Once we had everything we needed (personas, user flows, question-and-answer content and a new face for Pop-in), we had to structure a conversation that made clear who Pop-in is, what she does and how to talk to her. I tried several platforms before finding the right one, which was also how I learned how chatbots work. We settled on DialogFlow for natural language understanding, and ManyChat to run the bot on Facebook Messenger, where parents already were.

We also needed a website to present our goals and values. After drawing the sitemap, I designed prototypes in Adobe XD to show parents and coaches how the platform would let them start a coaching programme, then launched it with Wix.

Beyond the chatbot

Our chatbot could answer more than 100 questions about sleep and baby health, and parents found answers quickly. But was that enough? Every child is different, and a scripted bot could not give truly personal advice.

Who is best placed to understand and solve a child's sleep problems? Specialists. Sleep coaches are common in many countries but little known in France, so we decided to bring baby sleep professionals into Pop-in, to guide parents when the chatbot reached its limits.

Looking back

Pop-in was built in 2019, before large language models. AI has progressed so much since then that a general assistant now answers these questions better than our scripted chatbot ever could.

Some of the work still holds, though: grounding every answer in science, designing a conversation people can trust, and knowing when to hand over to a human expert. These are exactly the questions designers face with AI assistants today.
