Apnea

A web app to train and track static apnea: max holds with contraction markers, CO₂ and O₂ tables, breathing exercises and progress charts. It installs on a phone, works offline and keeps everything on the device.

Open Apnea

Most freediving progress happens on dry land, lying on a couch with a stopwatch. The trouble is that a stopwatch only tells you how long you held. It says nothing about when the urge to breathe started, how the hold felt, or whether last month's training made any difference.

Apnea started as a better stopwatch and grew into a small training companion, built for one-handed use with your eyes closed.

Listening to the contractions

A max hold has three phases: a guided relaxation, the hold itself, and a recovery. During the hold, the whole bottom of the screen becomes one big button: tap it at each diaphragm contraction. Each tap leaves a mark on the ring.

Afterwards, the app separates the calm part of the hold from the fight phase, the time between the first contraction and the end. Over the weeks, watching that first contraction arrive later is often a better sign of progress than the record itself.

Tables, breathing and dives

CO₂ tables keep the same hold and shorten the rest each round; O₂ tables do the opposite. Both can be built from your personal best, and the whole plan is shown before you start. A breathing section offers box breathing, 4-7-8, coherence or your own pattern, with an animated ring and voice cues.

Dives from a Garmin watch can be imported straight from a .fit or .csv file. I wrote the FIT decoder from scratch, so the app still has no dependency at all.

What I learned

Timing turned out to be the hard part. A browser happily slows down a hidden tab, so the timers follow the wall clock rather than counting frames, and the screen is kept awake during a session. Voice and sound cues let you train with your eyes closed.

Everything is stored in your browser: no account, no server, no tracking.

Safety first: this app is made for dry training. Never hold your breath in or near water without a trained buddy watching you.
